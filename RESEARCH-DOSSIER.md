## Positioning — why the site says what it says

Ru charges $60 for a haircut in a market where FunKing Barber and Elegant Barber Shop (both Hollywood, both with real websites and service-page SEO) charge $40. He is not competing on price and should not try to. What he actually sells, in his own words on Square, is an **Experience**: every cut includes a deep-cleansing shampoo, a deep-tissue hot-towel face massage and precise razor work, in a private suite, by appointment only, with one barber.

So the site's job is to make the $60 feel *cheap for what it is*. Hence:

- "The Ritual" — the six steps, made visible, before the menu is ever shown.
- "The difference" table — the usual shop vs the RuCutz Experience, line by line.
- Prices published everywhere (competitors bury them or hide behind "text for pricing").
- Locs-to-waves big chops as a signature: a $100, 75-minute service with almost no local competition and high emotional stakes.

## SEO / AEO / GEO plan

- Unique title + description + canonical + OG on every page; `HairSalon` + `Person` + `Service` + `FAQPage` + `BreadcrumbList` + `Article` JSON-LD, all cross-linked by `@id`.
- Answer-first FAQ copy: every answer opens with the direct answer and names the business, so it can be lifted as a snippet or by an assistant.
- `/llms.txt` publishes the NAP, hours, full price list and every FAQ answer as plain text for AI answer engines.
- Three long-form guides targeting real queries ("how to get 360 waves", "what to expect from a big chop", "why barbers shampoo first"), each opening with a one-paragraph answer.
- Local entity consistency: the same NAP string everywhere, `areaServed` for the ten cities Ru actually draws from, and `sameAs` links to every profile he owns.
